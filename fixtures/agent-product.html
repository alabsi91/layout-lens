The Marlowe began as a pattern we found in a Ministry of Supply catalogue from 1948, cut for dispatch riders who needed a coat that would survive a winter of bad weather and worse roads. We kept the long body and the bellows pockets, dropped the wool storm collar, and moved the hand warmers up two inches so they land where your hands actually sit.

The shell is a ten-wale waxed cotton milled in Lancashire. It arrives stiff and a little unfriendly. Six weeks in, it holds the shape of your shoulders and the creases at your elbows, and it stops looking like something bought and starts looking like something owned.

"A jacket should be worse in the shop than it is after a year. This one is."

The liner

A quilted lambswool liner buttons in along the placket and at both cuffs. On its own it wears as a vest under a suit. Buttoned in, the jacket handles about twenty degrees colder than the shell alone, which covers most of the year almost anywhere with a real autumn.

Fit

Cut straight through the body with room for a heavy knit. If you are between sizes and plan to wear the liner most days, take the larger one. The sleeves run long by design and can be shortened at any of our shops at no charge.

Care

Never wash it. Brush the dirt off with a stiff brush and cold water. Re-wax once a year, or every second year if it lives mostly indoors, using the tin we include in the box.
